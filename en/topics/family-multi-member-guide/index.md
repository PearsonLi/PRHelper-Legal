<section class="story-panel"><p class="story-kicker">One family, three different timelines</p><h2>The same flight does not create the same application record.</h2><p>Priya, Arjun and their daughter travelled together, but their PR dates and citizenship plans differ. Copying one spreadsheet across all three hides those differences. The safer workflow keeps each person separate, then reviews the household together.</p></section>

<div class="direct-answer"><h2>How should a family organize Canadian residency records?</h2><p>Keep each person’s status date, trips, planning date and supporting evidence separate. Use a household view only for coordination; do not merge individual eligibility timelines into one total.</p></div>

<h2>Question 1: How many members are supported?</h2>
<p>Up to 10 members are currently supported.</p>

<h2>Question 2: Are member settings independent?</h2>
<p>Yes. Each member has independent PR settings and trip records.</p>

<h2>Question 3: How are exports organized?</h2>
<p>One file with separated per-member sections, headers, and pagination.</p>

<h2>Choose Family only when a second timeline is the real block</h2>
<p>Stay with an individual workflow while one person is all you need. Consider multi-member access when another person needs a separate PR date, travel history or planning result—not simply because two people share a home.</p>

<div class="callout"><strong>Independent planning tool:</strong> each person’s final application must still be verified against current official instructions.</div>
