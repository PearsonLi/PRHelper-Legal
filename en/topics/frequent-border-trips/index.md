<h2>High-volume travel needs a review system</h2>
<p>For someone who crosses the border a few times a year, a calendar may be enough. For weekly crossings, the risk is cumulative: similar weekends blur together, land crossings may leave no passport stamp, and one wrong return date can overlap the next trip.</p>

<h2>A workflow that scales</h2>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Capture quickly.</strong> Record the crossing after returning or process a small weekly batch.</li>
  <li><strong>Pair each exit with a return.</strong> Do not leave unpaired dates hidden in a long list.</li>
  <li><strong>Use a consistent source hierarchy.</strong> Start with official records when available, then compare bookings, calendars, tolls, fuel, card transactions, and location evidence.</li>
  <li><strong>Flag same-day movements.</strong> Keep them visible so the final application instructions can be applied correctly.</li>
  <li><strong>Run a monthly gap review.</strong> Look for overlaps, impossible sequences, and long periods with no activity.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Separate recording from legal calculation</h2>
<p>Your permanent raw record should contain the actual movements you can support. The way a particular application counts or asks you to report those movements can change by program and form. Keeping the source timeline complete makes it easier to apply the correct official rules later.</p>

<div class="callout"><strong>Do not optimize the record for a desired result.</strong> Record what happened, retain the source, and use current IRCC instructions or licensed advice for the application decision.</div>
