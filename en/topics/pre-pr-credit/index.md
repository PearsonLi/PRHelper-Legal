<div class="direct-answer"><h2>Can time in Canada before PR count toward citizenship?</h2><p>Some eligible time as a temporary resident or protected person inside the relevant five-year period may count as one-half day, up to a maximum credit of 365 days. It applies to citizenship physical presence, not the PR residency obligation.</p></div>

<section class="story-panel"><p class="story-kicker">The moving-window problem</p><h2>A year in Canada before PR does not stay in the calculation forever.</h2><p>Leah became a permanent resident after studying in Canada. When she moves her planned citizenship date, the five-year window moves too. The useful record is not a single saved credit total; it is the dates and status behind every potentially eligible period.</p></section>

<h2>Keep the inputs needed for verification</h2>
<ol><li>Record each period in Canada before PR and the immigration status held.</li><li>Keep evidence for the start and end dates.</li><li>Recalculate when the intended application date changes.</li><li>Use the official IRCC calculator for the final application result.</li></ol>
