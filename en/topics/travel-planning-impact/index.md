<h2>A ticket changes more than a calendar</h2>
<p>A planned absence can move a citizenship application date, reduce a PR residency margin, or affect family members differently. The useful time to discover that impact is before a non-refundable booking—not after returning.</p>

<h2>Test three dates, not one total</h2>
<ol>
  <li><strong>The departure date.</strong> Confirm the record is current immediately before leaving.</li>
  <li><strong>The intended return date.</strong> Add the whole planned absence, including possible travel-day changes.</li>
  <li><strong>The future decision date.</strong> Review the date that matters: a PR-card application, a citizenship application, or another official assessment.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Review PR and citizenship separately</h2>
<p>The two timelines can react differently to the same trip. PR compliance uses the applicable residency-obligation period. Citizenship physical presence uses its own five-year period and may include eligible pre-PR credit. A single reassuring total should not replace two separate checks.</p>

<h2>Plan with a buffer</h2>
<p>Flights change, records are corrected, and intended application dates move. A plan that works only on an exact boundary is fragile. Use official tools to verify the final result, and consider whether extra in-Canada time creates a more resilient plan.</p>

<h2>Pay for planning only when a real booking decision is blocked</h2>
<p>First keep the current timeline accurate with the free record. Consider an advanced planning option only when you need to compare a specific future absence or evaluation date before committing money to travel. Define that trip before opening the plan screen.</p>

<div class="callout"><strong>Planning signals are not legal determinations.</strong> They depend on the dates entered. Verify final eligibility with current IRCC guidance and seek licensed advice where the margin is close or an exception matters.</div>
